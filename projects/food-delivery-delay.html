Food Delivery Delay Analysis

SQL · RELATIONAL DATABASE DESIGN · HTML DASHBOARD

Everyone blames traffic for a late order. I wanted to know if that was actually true, or just the easiest excuse — so I built a database to find out.

View repository ↗

Delivery delays are usually blamed on one thing (traffic, obviously) without anyone actually checking. So instead of accepting the folklore, I modeled the problem properly: orders, restaurants, time slots, and delay reasons, all connected in a relational schema, then queried it until the real pattern showed up.

What I built

  • Designed a custom relational database schema — structured tables for orders, restaurants, delivery time slots, and delay reasons — instead of dumping everything into one flat sheet.
  • Wrote SQL queries to analyze delay patterns by restaurant, time slot, and stated reason.
  • Built an interactive HTML dashboard to make the delay breakdown visual instead of a wall of query output.
  • Found that traffic really is the biggest culprit — accounting for 50% of delays — but it's not the only story worth telling.

Why it matters

Once delay reasons are structured data instead of a vague complaint field, a delivery operation can actually act on them — reroute during known traffic windows, staff up at the restaurants that consistently run slow, or renegotiate time slot promises with customers.
